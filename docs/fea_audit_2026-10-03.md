Branch `fea-loop`. Every number below was produced on 2026-10-03 and can be re-derived
from the files named next to it. **CONFIRMED** = run and measured today. **ASSUMED** =

Raw results: `runs/loop_eff_2026-10-03/` (loop), `runs/bench/*.jsonl` (one line per

## 0. A correction to the premise

`plateau_stress_kPa` was NaN in all 133 rows, but the **loop never optimized on that
column**. `fea_optimize.py` computes its objectives from the saved curves:

| iterations | stress objective actually minimized |
|---|---|
| 1-10  | peak stress up to 20% (`hv_reference.json.superseded`) |
| 11-25 | mean stress over 0.10-0.20 (`hv_reference.json`) |

So "the loop minimized stress@20%" holds for iterations 1-10 only. The collapse onto
density came from pairing that stress with **SEA**, not from the NaN column.
(CONFIRMED, from the two reference files and `run_sea20.log`.)

There are also two different efficiency definitions in the repo:

- **Loop** (`fea_optimize`): η = ∫σdε / (σ_peak · ε).
- **Rescore script and the brief:** η = ∫σdε / (σ(ε) · ε).

They agree on monotonic curves, but 12 of the 27 usable curves soften after a peak
(it005_0: 0.819 by peak vs 0.874 by σ(ε)). **I kept the peak form.** It is the
standard Avalle definition and a true ≤ 1 bound. The σ(ε) form can exceed the ideal
rectangle, and it rewards a load spike the head would feel. *This departs from the
formula in the brief.* Switching costs one line, but it changes the objective, so it
is your call.

## 2. Plateau / densification definition — CONFIRMED

- **Plateau band is now relative to the crush.** `fea_evaluator.PLATEAU_BAND = (0.5,
  1.0) × crush`: 0.10-0.20 for the screening piece, 0.05-0.10 for the 10% full tile.
  The old band was absolute 0.20-0.40, which no solve ever reaches.
- **One shared, strain-weighted mean.** `band_mean()` replaces the per-sample mean,
  which over-weighted the solver's crowded cutback points. The loop objective now
  imports the same function, so the master table and the objective are one number
  (it026_5: 64.49 in both).
- **Every row carries its band** (`plateau_band_lo/hi`).
  `schema.check_plateau_bands` refuses to mix bands, exactly as `check_sea_depths`
  refuses to mix SEA depths, and it fires on the current master table (piece and tile
  mixed). `check_sea_depths` is unchanged.
- **New rows:** the plateau is populated for every design that reached its crush and
  NaN only for stalled solves.
- **Densification is still NaN, and correctly so.** No design densifies by 20%, so the
  efficiency peak lies beyond the crush. Measuring it needs a deeper crush, which the
  static solve cannot reach (§5).

## 3. FEA setup audit — CONFIRMED unless marked

| check | result |
|---|---|
| Units | N, mm, MPa. Single-material cube to 10%: 313.5 N on 100 mm², between small-strain E (280 N) and incompressible NH (335 N). Neo-Hooke C10 5.005 / D1 0.0435 → G 10.0, K 46.0, E 28.0 MPa, **ν 0.40**. |
| Stress / strain | Nominal: σ = RF_z / (bbox Lx·Ly), ε = δ / H, H = mesh z-extent. Consistent with the cube. |
| Relative density | **From mesh volume.** Tet volume / bbox = STL volume / bbox, to 4 decimals on 6 designs, within 1% of the requested density. No sliver volume lost. SEA mass uses the same value. |
| Dimensions | **The "2×2×2 piece" is not a fixed size.** H ranges 25-76 mm across designs. it009_2's piece is **76 mm tall, twice the 38.1 mm liner**: 2 cells through the thickness where the real tile has 1. The likely cause of the larger piece-vs-tile offsets on 20.4 mm cells (it004_0 -24%). |
| Platen BC | Every node within **2% of H** (0.5-1.5 mm, about one wall) of the top or bottom is glued in z. Moving it to 0.2% changes thin-walled results **10-25%** (blend plateau 179.5 → 134.9 kPa). Gyroid: 2-3%. |
| Symmetry planes | The validation was on a solid specimen, where the planes are exact. On a lattice piece cut at an arbitrary plane they are an assumption. Measured below. |

Symmetry vs free sides vs bonded platens, 20% crush (`runs/bench/bc.jsonl`):

| benchmark | BC | σ@2.5% | σ@10% | plateau 10-20% | η |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gyroid ρ .20 | sym (loop) | 63.8 | 250.5 | 365.7 | 0.52 |
| | free sides | 61.0 | 236.1 | 340.7 | 0.53 |
| | bonded | — | 263.7 | — | — |
| blend it005_0 | sym (loop) | 74.0 | 184.6 | **179.5** | 0.82 |
| | free sides | 72.0 | 61.5 | **47.5** | 0.70 |
| | bonded | — | 177.2 | — | — |

- **Parity check:** the `sym` deck reproduces the loop's stored it005_0 plateau
  (179.5 vs 179.5 kPa), so each variant differs from the loop's deck by one knob only.
- **Free sides:** the blend peaks at 3.8% and collapses.
- **Bonded platens:** the collapse disappears. So it is a **whole-piece sway** made
  possible by lateral platen slip, not local wall buckling.

Conclusion:

- The symmetry planes **do** suppress an asymmetric mode. But that mode is a
  piece-scale sway that a 60-cell-wide tile bonded to a shell would not freely
  undergo.
- On these two benchmarks the loop's BC sits within 4-5% of the bonded bound.
- The auxetic-specific question is **not answered**. Neither benchmark is
  auxetic-dominant, and a periodic RVE BC would be the right test.

**Rigid-platen contact.**

- Implemented as `fea_variants`, `bc="contact"`: rigid C3D8 platens and
  surface-to-surface contact, frictionless or Coulomb.
- **Validated on a cube:** frictionless matches the free BC within 0.07%; μ = 0.3
  adds 12% (barreling).
- **On the blend** (frictionless): 68.7 vs 74.0 kPa at 2.5%, so the glued slab adds
  about 7% even at small strain. It then stalled at 3.9%, the same sway onset.
- The gyroid and μ = 0.3 lattice cases **did not run**. Claude Code stopped them under
  memory pressure while three solves were running at once. **NOT DONE.**
- Self-contact is not modelled.

## 4. Elements, mesh convergence, compressibility — CONFIRMED (gyroid), PARTIAL (blend)

Mesh: 10% crush, loop BC (`runs/bench/mesh.jsonl`). Times are wall-clock. Chain
cases ran on 4 threads alongside the loop; the quoted uncontended number used 12.

| case | elems | σ@2.5% | σ@10% kPa | time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gyroid C3D4 res60 (loop) | 38k | 63.8 | **250.5** | 39 s |
| gyroid C3D4 res90 | 93k | 59.7 | 230.8 | 145 s |
| gyroid C3D4 res120 | 194k | 58.4 | 223.8 | 623 s |
| gyroid C3D10 res60 | 38k (72k nodes) | 52.9 | **197.7** | 778 s uncontended |
| gyroid C3D10 res90 | 93k (171k nodes) | 53.4 | 199.2 | 4042 s |
| blend C3D4 res60 (loop) | 59k | 73.9 | 172.6 | 77 s |
| blend C3D4 res90 | 146k | 74.6 | 124.8 | 315 s |
| blend C3D4 res120 | 284k | 75.3 | 137.9 | 1536 s |
| blend C3D10 res60 | 59k (111k nodes) | 73.0 | — (timed out at 4.2%) | 5400 s |

**Gyroid.** C3D10 converges at the loop's own pitch (res60 vs res90: 0.7%). The loop's
C3D4 res60 is **+27% stiff** at 10% crush. C3D4 res120 is still +12% stiff, at a cost
comparable to C3D10 res60 (623 s on 4 threads vs 778 s on 12). **C3D10 res60 is the accurate option, at about 19× the
loop's solve time.**

**Blend.**

- **Below the peak it is converged:** σ@2.5% spans 73-75 kPa across every mesh and
  both elements.
- **Past the peak (about 5-8%) the results are path- and mesh-dependent.** The C3D4
  series is non-monotonic (172.6 → 124.8 → 137.9). The same mesh read 184.6 at 10%
  when crushed to 20% with larger increments.
- **Refining by marching-cubes pitch also changes the wall geometry** (5th-percentile
  wall 0.43 → 0.35 mm). So that series cannot isolate mesh error on thin walls. A
  fixed-geometry volume remesh is needed and is **not done**.

**Mesh quality** (all meshes):

- The worst tet sits right at the 1e-4 sliver cut-off (a regular tet is 0.118).
- The thinnest blend walls get 0.4-0.6 elements across.
- C3D10 Jacobians are **positive everywhere** (min 3.5e-9).
- The C3D10s are straight-sided (midside nodes at edge midpoints), so the Jacobian
  equals the parent tet's. No curved-element inversion is possible, and a full
  quadratic-shape-function check confirmed it.

Compressibility: C10 fixed, D1 alone, 10% crush (`runs/bench/compress.jsonl`). ν never
reaches 0.5, since CalculiX's C3D10 is not hybrid.

| ν (D1) | gyroid C3D4 | gyroid C3D10 | blend C3D4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.400 (0.0435, current) | 250.5 | 197.7 | 172.6 (peaks, softens) |
| 0.478 (0.0087) | 307.0 (+23%) | 215.5 (+9%) | 265.0 (+54%, no peak) |
| 0.489 (0.00435) | 339.3 (+35%) | 219.2 (+11%) | 308.2 (+79%, no peak) |

- **Physical effect** (C3D10): about +11% and levelling off.
- **C3D4 locks:** its error over C3D10 grows from 27% to 55%.
- **On the blend the bulk modulus changes the buckling behaviour qualitatively.** At
  ν ≥ 0.478 the peak and softening disappear. Part of that is C3D4 locking stiffening
  the walls against buckling.
- **The real ν is unmeasured** (ASSUMED 0.40 via D1 0.0435). The efficiency ranking of
  buckling-prone designs therefore currently depends on an assumed D1.

## 5. Implicit dynamics — PARTIAL, and the result is negative

- **Implemented:** `fea_variants.dynamic_step`.
  - `*DYNAMIC, ALPHA=-0.05` (implicit HHT; no DIRECT or EXPLICIT).
  - Smooth C2 displacement ramp; tonne/mm³ density.
  - KE and internal energy from `*EL PRINT ELSE, ELKE`.
  - No STABILIZE anywhere.
- **Cube check:** KE/IE = 1e-8. CONFIRMED quasi-static.
- **Blend, free sides:** reached 10.7%, then "increment size smaller than minimum".
  KE/IE peaked at **0.49**. That is a genuine dynamic snap-through at the sway
  collapse, which by definition fails a quasi-static check.
- **it027_1** (a loop design whose static solve stalls at 5.4%, loop BC): HHT stalled
  **earlier**, at 4.2%, with KE/IE 0.11.
  - The static curve stalls with stress **still rising** (166.9 kPa), not at a limit
    point. So the stall is **element-level** (sliver / thin-wall distortion), not
    structural buckling. Dynamics cannot fix that.
- **The dt / duration / α validation sweep was not completed.** Both benchmarks fail
  at the first case, so halving dt cannot validate a run that never gets through.
  **NOT DONE.**
- **Recommendation:** fix the mesh first (remesh the surface to a uniform size with at
  least 2 elements across the thinnest wall, then volume-mesh), then retry dynamics.

## 6. Shear load case — CONFIRMED (code + benchmarks), UNVALIDATED (physics)

- **Implemented:** `fea_evaluator.evaluate_shear`, a separate solve on the same mesh.
  - Bonded platens; top driven tangentially at constant thickness; free sides.
  - Reports τ(γ), the secant shear modulus at γ = 0.10, and the initial tangent.
  - Writes `shear_validated=False` on every row.
- **Loop flag:** `run_loop.py --shear` adds it as a **third, independent** objective
  (minimize τ at γ = 0.10). It never mixes with compression. A shear solve failure
  leaves that objective NaN; it is never read as zero.
- **Benchmarks** (`runs/bench/shear.jsonl`), τ at γ = 0.10:

  | benchmark | τ at γ = 0.10 | G_eff | solve |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | gyroid | 82.3 kPa | ≈ 0.82 MPa | 35 s, clean |
  | blend | 31.7 kPa | ≈ 0.32 MPa | 57 s, clean |

- **Not yet used in a loop run.** It needs a fresh run, since objectives cannot change
  mid-run.
- **No physical shear rig exists**, so the error is unknown. Expect it to be larger
  than in compression.
- **ASSUMED:** minimizing shear stiffness alone favours low density, so it may
  correlate with plateau stress. Measure that before trusting a 3-objective front.

  - is not `"source": "lab"`;

  Verified: a published-source manifest is rejected.

  writes no card.
- **It has not been run on any real or published coefficients.**

## What I'd decide next (yours to call)

1. **Mesh quality first.** It is the common root cause of the static stalls, the
   failed dynamics, and the steering away from buckling designs.
2. **Switch screening to C3D10** only if the ~19× cost is acceptable. Otherwise apply a
   documented +27%-style C3D4 correction, which is valid only for the gyroid class and
   only pre-peak.
3. **Measure ν (DIC lateral strain)** before trusting buckling-driven efficiency
   rankings.
4. **Fix the piece height to the liner** (1 cell through 38.1 mm when the cell snaps
   that way) so piece and tile are the same structure.
